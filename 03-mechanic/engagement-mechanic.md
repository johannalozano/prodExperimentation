# The Mechanic · FinWise

> Module 3 · Retention & Engagement. The engagement mechanic that builds the habit loop, with rationale and a wireframe.

## The mechanic

**Mechanic:** Progress Bar — **Weekly Financial Health Check**

**Behavior reinforced:**  
A small-business owner returns weekly to review current financial health, identify what needs attention, and refresh their near-term financial forecast.

**Habit loop:**

**Trigger**  
A new week begins or FinWise detects a meaningful change in the user’s financial data.

**Action**  
The user completes the Weekly Financial Health Check:
- Review current cash position
- Check unpaid invoices
- Review upcoming expenses
- Refresh the 30-day financial forecast

**Reward**  
FinWise confirms that the user’s financial picture is up to date and surfaces a meaningful financial outcome, such as:
- “No critical cash-flow issues detected this week.”
- “Your 30-day forecast has been refreshed.”
- “Cash remains above your safety threshold.”

**Investment**  
The user updates or resolves financial items and refreshes their forecast, which makes the next weekly review more accurate and useful.

## Rationale

FinWise’s 60% annual churn appears more likely to be **value- and engagement-related than price-related**.

The Lab 1 usage data shows that financial-modeling usage can be relatively high, reaching above 50% in several months, while average session duration can fall to roughly 5–8 minutes. This suggests that users may reach valuable features like financial modeling without consistently building a deeper, recurring workflow around them.

The **Weekly Financial Health Check** is designed to turn those valuable but potentially intermittent behaviors into a repeatable weekly habit. The progress bar gives users a clear sense of completion, while each step reconnects them with FinWise’s core value: understanding cash position, reviewing financial risks, and updating forecasts.

The reward is intentionally not points, badges, or an arbitrary score. The reward is **financial clarity and control**.

This mechanic should improve retention if the churn problem is caused by users failing to make FinWise part of their regular financial-management routine.

However, if users are already reviewing forecasts but do not find the insights useful or actionable enough, the progress mechanic alone will not solve the underlying retention problem. In that case, FinWise would need to improve the quality and usefulness of the financial insights themselves.
